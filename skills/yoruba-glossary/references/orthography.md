# Orthography, àmì and pronunciation

Use for spelling, tone marking, pronunciation notation or correction in Standard Yorùbá; do not regularize dialects, personal names or exact identifiers.

Check underdotted letters separately from tone marks. Distinguish a nasalized vowel from a syllabic nasal that bears its own tone. Meaning and grammatical context determine readings; bare text cannot reliably recover lexical or contextual tone, contractions or connected-speech changes.

Keep these distinctions concrete when teaching: `a e ẹ i o ọ u` are the seven oral vowel letters; the underdots in `ẹ`, `ọ` and `ṣ` distinguish letters, not tones. High tone uses an acute accent, low uses a grave, and mid is normally unmarked. The checked contrast `bí` (give birth), `bi` (ask), `bì` (vomit) shows why an unmarked tone is not an absent tone. Teach relative pitch rather than English stress or fixed musical notes; the contrast is from Yorùbá Yé Mi, printed p. 17.

Unicode NFC preserves canonical representation but neither supplies missing tone nor repairs the wrong letter. Check underdots and tone through copying/rendering, preserve quotations and names exactly, and record proposed corrections separately. Legacy-font PDFs/pages require rendered inspection or another reliable source before copying a suspect glyph.

Pronunciation guidance from text is approximate. Use verified audio when available and never claim to assess a learner's spoken tone from spelling alone.
