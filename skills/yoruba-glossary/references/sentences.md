# Sentence composition

Start from intended meaning, speaker/addressee, register and context rather than substituting words into English syntax.

Use basic SVO, noun-modifier and pronoun patterns only as starting points. Modifiers have exceptions; plural and honorific interpretation depend on context; subject, object, possessive and emphatic pronouns are not interchangeable. Time reference uses particles, aspect and context rather than English-style verb inflection, and a bare verb is not automatically past.

Negation and questions vary by pronoun, aspect, focus and construction. Do not generalize one particle or example into a full paradigm. Check source-backed patterns before extending to serial verbs, relative clauses, focus or other constructions.

Preserve idiom, register, word boundaries and intended meaning. Distinguish correction from optional style. Return the sentence, a natural translation when useful, and the smallest reusable explanation; a word-by-word gloss is structural evidence, not necessarily idiomatic translation.

Sources: University of Georgia [Yoruba Online grammar](https://africa.uga.edu/Yoruba/grammar.html), an introductory overview with legacy-font limits; UT Austin COERLL [Yorùbá Yé Mi chapter 1](https://coerll.utexas.edu/yemi/pdfs/yy_ch1.pdf) for checked negation/question examples and [chapter 2](https://coerll.utexas.edu/yemi/pdfs/yy_ch2.pdf) for possessives, plurality and honorific context.
