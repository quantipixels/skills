# Plan an outcome

Read [planning inputs](../references/productivity/planning/planning-inputs.md). Create or revise a practical approach to reaching and verifying an agreed outcome. Own planning, not initiative execution. A planning-only request ends at the plan; when called by [atona](atona.md), return the plan and controlling gaps so that caller can continue.

## Recover what is settled

Reuse the supplied brief, current plan, accepted decisions and relevant project evidence. Establish the outcome, acceptance, scope/non-goals and requested planning depth. Resolve discoverable facts before asking the user; distinguish established requirements from proposals and assumptions. Never start a second plan over a current one merely because another method authored it.

Turn a rough request into a concrete goal and observable finish using that context. Account for necessary in-scope steps and dependencies the user omitted. Where competing interpretations would materially change the outcome or scope, state what you understand and ask for the controlling clarification; do not invent a human preference to make the plan look complete.

When one idea still needs shaping, use [arojinle](arojinle.md) for consequential unresolved purpose or trade-offs and [seda-spec](seda-spec.md) when observable behavior needs a contract. Use [atona-direction](atona-direction.md) when credible alternatives are needed, rather than brainstorming again over a settled choice. Use [iwadi](iwadi.md) for material external evidence gaps. Return unresolved prerequisites to the caller instead of inventing decisions.

When selecting or changing the development approach, read [SDLC adaptation](../references/engineering/development-practice.md) to fit depth, capabilities, feedback and recovery to confirmed project philosophy. Reuse an adequate existing working path; no universal stage sequence is required.

## Establish enough approach and sequence

Keep the outcome and acceptance, decisions and material assumptions, delivery approach and sequence, dependencies, risks, verification, documentation consequences and next action in one plan. Match detail to what the next contributor would otherwise have to invent. A short plan can remain in chat; use [records](../references/productivity/records.md) when continuity, collaboration or the requested deliverable needs a file.

Make the goal and current path easy to recover: destination, actual state, next ready result, and the evidence or decision that controls what follows. Long-running end-to-end work retains this anchor in its living plan. Keep enough path to guide the next action even when only the next investigation is knowable; neither a vague task list nor an invented exhaustive backlog is a substitute.

Use [amose](amose.md) for unsettled domain meaning or invariant applicability; [architect-design](architect-design.md) for consequential technical structure or mechanism fitness; and [seda-tickets](seda-tickets.md) for useful delivery decomposition. Consume their current results rather than copying their methods or requiring each branch. A specification owns behavior, architecture owns structure, and work packages own bounded tasks; the plan connects them without duplicating their records.

Identify how acceptance will be established before treating a slice as ready to build. Preserve required project checks and the user's division of work. Mark unavailable prerequisites and explicitly deferred proof; proposed checks are not executed evidence. Include material rollout checks, stop conditions and recovery obligations through [rollout readiness](../references/engineering/delivery/rollout-readiness.md) when operational acceptance or data recovery matters.

For consequential, uncertain, difficult-to-reverse or materially coordinated work, run a [premortem](../references/productivity/planning/premortem.md) and reconcile its material findings before the affected plan is execution-ready. Keep rationale, alternatives and counterevidence where they change decisions; distinguish proposed, confirmed, deferred and superseded choices. Trace acceptance to design, delivery and proof owners when that traceability is needed.

## Find the way through uncertainty

When the destination is recognizable but later work cannot yet be stated responsibly, read [progressive shaping](../references/productivity/planning/progressive-shaping.md). Choose the next evidence-producing question instead of inventing a complete backlog. Distinguish a known blocked question from territory whose questions depend on earlier answers.

Identify independently ready slices and keep unresolved dependent scope visible. Slice readiness does not establish whole-initiative readiness. A planning assignment may inspect and research, but does not authorize production changes, live operations or a prototype merely to fill a gap. Identify the needed experiment and its authority; an executing caller can obtain that result and return it for replanning.

For each material result, update the affected plan, retire invalid assumptions and reopen only dependent decisions and proof. Keep other recommendations optional unless they affect acceptance or a governing obligation. Formal readiness states apply only when the governing workflow requires [managed initiatives](../references/productivity/planning/managed-initiative.md); a planning result does not advance execution status on its own.

## Return a usable plan

Check that the approach fits the confirmed outcome, dependencies are genuine, each ready slice has sufficient acceptance and a credible verification path, and missing decisions remain explicit. State whether the plan is ready for the requested scope, partially ready with named independent slices, or blocked on a controlling prerequisite. A plan's existence is not readiness.

Present the plan's decision-changing obligations and limits in the requested format. Use [html-artifact](html-artifact.md) only for a requested browser document or when it materially helps the decision; the plan owns meaning and the artifact method owns construction and presentation proof. Keep the identity and relevant revision current when contributors need them.

Return the plan or its locator, readiness, source/evidence identities, assumptions and remaining decisions. For a standalone planning request, stop without implementing, publishing tickets or starting delivery. For an executing caller, return the result to that caller without a next-step permission menu; it retains continuation and authority.
