# Incident recovery

Use to mitigate an active disruption and establish recovery. A request only to explain a failure uses Investigation; a finished event needing a postmortem goes directly to `ayewo-igba-ise`.

1. Give `alaga` the current impact, environment, available observations, applicable runbook and execution authority. Its incident-recovery method owns bounded mitigation and recovery evidence; use the project's operational capability for live actions and report any material readiness gap.
2. Let the next observable recovery decision determine the probe or intervention. A supported reversible mitigation can precede a confirmed cause. Keep decisive evidence and update the existing incident record without making a new initiative plan a prerequisite to restoration.
3. Verify the affected user boundary and relevant data, backlog or duplicate-effect consequences. If recovery fails, return the actual result and stop condition to `alaga`; do not blindly replay a command whose effects are uncertain.
4. Report restored, degraded or still-disrupted service separately from causal certainty. Continue permanent repair or a postmortem when requested or included in the incident scope. `alaga` owns repair, `ayewo-igba-ise` owns the completed event's judgment, and `atona` coordinates larger accepted follow-up work.

Finish at the requested recovery state with evidence, residual impact and the next responsible action. An accepted mitigation, healthy process or successful rollback command alone is not proof of recovered service.
