# Asset organization

Use the project's existing asset layout, naming, storage, and approval convention. Do not impose a QP directory tree, manifest, filename formula, tag taxonomy, date scheme, file-size threshold, or archive policy.

## Inspect before organizing

Identify the current asset roots, source-versus-export separation, naming patterns, version control or external storage, manifest or DAM integration, ownership, provenance, approval state, and consumers. Preserve conventions that are current and usable. Record contradictions or missing ownership before moving or renaming files.

## Choose only needed structure

Add structure only when it resolves a concrete retrieval, provenance, approval, delivery, or lifecycle problem. Prefer the project's native repository, DAM, Figma, cloud-storage, or build metadata over a parallel registry.

For each asset, retain only metadata that a real consumer needs, such as:

- stable identity and exact path or provider ID;
- source and export relationship;
- dimensions, format, color profile, or crop when operationally relevant;
- provenance, license, owner, approval state, and approved use; and
- version or replacement link when the workflow requires it.

Do not invent values when evidence is absent. Treat generated-media provenance and external-storage links according to the project's privacy, licensing, and retention rules.

## Verify a change

Before a move or rename, find current consumers and confirm mutation authority. Afterward, read back the exact destination, verify required consumers and manifests, and report unresolved references. Never delete or archive an asset without explicit authority and a recoverable plan.
