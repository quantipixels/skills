# Language and messaging

Use when Brand must define, update, or review how the brand speaks or what it claims. Ground the contract in current audience, positioning, product truth, approved language, existing high-confidence examples, required notices, and evidence for every material claim; do not begin from a generic personality workshop or messaging formula.

## Establish the language contract

Record only what current evidence supports:

- **Audience and context** — who is being addressed and in which material situations/channels.
- **Voice qualities** — stable communication qualities with evidence-backed positive/negative boundaries.
- **Terminology** — preferred product/domain names, prohibited/confusing synonyms, and localization constraints.
- **Tone adaptations** — only contextual shifts that the brand actually needs; voice remains recognisable across them.
- **Claims and promises** — exact statements the brand may make, their proof source, scope, and material caveats.
- **Positioning/differentiation** — only when approved evidence establishes a real comparison or distinct value.
- **Action language** — CTA/imperative conventions where the product/brand has an established pattern.
- **Prohibited language** — misleading, unsupported, legally/ethically constrained, or explicitly off-brand treatments.

Do not require a fixed number of traits, supporting messages, proof points, elevator pitches, audience segments, or tone spectra. Do not invent mission/vision/value-proposition sentences merely to fill a brand template.

## Shape messages from evidence

For each material message, keep the relationship explicit:

```text
Audience/job
What they need to understand or do
Source-supported claim/message
Proof or product truth
Material caveat/constraint
Desired action when applicable
```

A concise message may omit fields that are irrelevant, but it may not strengthen the evidence. Distinguish approved copy from a proposed wording candidate.

## Review

Compare the exact copy/surface with the current contract. Report:

- contradicted or unsupported claim;
- terminology/voice/tone mismatch;
- audience/context mismatch;
- missing caveat or required notice;
- smallest correction; and
- evidence or approval needed when the right wording is not yet established.

Do not reject copy because it fails a generic brand archetype, adjective count, pitch formula, or competitor-differentiation test that the actual brand never adopted.
