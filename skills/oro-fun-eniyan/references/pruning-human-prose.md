# Pruning human prose

Read only when the user explicitly asks to shorten prose, reduce repetition, or lower reading load.

Preserve facts, evidence, citations, decisions, uncertainty, identifiers, required structure, and intended voice. A shorter document is not better if the reader must reconstruct missing context or authority.

## Prune in this order

1. Remove filler, throat-clearing, repeated conclusions, and summaries that add no new fact or action.
2. Merge explanations with genuinely identical meaning.
3. Move background only when the artifact has a clear linked home and the main reader job remains intact.
4. Remove rationale that does not help the reader decide or understand a non-obvious boundary.
5. Remove examples that neither replace explanation nor prevent a plausible misunderstanding.
6. Prefer shorter familiar constructions, then repair rhythm and clarity.

Keep repetition when it serves a different audience need, warning, exception, or decision boundary.

## Verify

Compare the revision with the exact baseline. Confirm that the argument or procedure still works, all evidence/citations remain attached to the right claims, and the voice still fits the artifact.

For material cuts, report the meaningful content moved or removed and any preservation limit. Word count can describe the change; it cannot prove quality.
