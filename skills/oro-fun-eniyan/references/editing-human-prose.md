# Editing human prose

Use for meaning-preserving cleanup of supplied human-facing prose. Keep facts, citations, exact identifiers, accepted terminology, required formats, and the artifact's decisions intact.

## Match the reader and voice

Identify the reader, purpose, language/locale, intended voice, and text that must remain exact. If a stylistic change would alter meaning or authority, flag it instead of hiding the change inside cleanup.

Sound direct and specific. Vary sentence length naturally. Keep reference material neutral unless the artifact calls for a voice. Do not add deliberate mess, personality, or opinion merely to appear human.

## Remove patterns by effect

Rewrite only when a pattern causes ambiguity, puffery, unsupported certainty, needless load, or a voice mismatch.

- Remove promotional significance claims and generic challenge-and-triumph framing.
- Replace vague attribution with the actual source or appropriately qualified evidence.
- Replace abstract padding and weak nominalizations with the concrete actor, mechanism, action, or result.
- Remove formulaic threes, false ranges, synonym cycling, and comparisons that do not change the point.
- Cut throat-clearing, repeated caveats, stacked modals, and assistant-performance language.
- Reduce decorative formatting when it substitutes for structure.

Keep a domain term when it is precise even if it sounds unusual. Do not rewrite exact facts or identifiers for style.

## Verify

Read the revision against the original:

1. Did any fact, citation, identifier, decision, status, or required field change?
2. Does each remaining sentence help the reader understand, decide, act, or verify?
3. Does the prose fit this artifact rather than a generic polished voice?
4. Is uncertainty still calibrated to the evidence?

Restore lost nuance. Return revised prose first and report only material meaning, evidence, or voice questions that remain.
