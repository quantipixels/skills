# Plan premortem

Use for consequential, uncertain, difficult-to-reverse or materially coordinated work before declaring the affected plan ready. Reuse a premortem only while its plan, assumptions, dependencies and evidence remain applicable. A simple reversible task needs none.

Pin the plan revision and assume delivery finished but the outcome failed or caused an unacceptable consequence. Work backward to credible causal paths: false assumptions, unsafe intermediate states, unavailable dependencies/permissions, broken handoffs, misleading verification, or unrecoverable effects. Include adoption, operational capacity, cost or timing only when they control the outcome. These are hypotheses, not incidents or measured probabilities.

Use an independent perspective when a consequential or contested assumption warrants it and the host supports one; otherwise do not claim independent review. Generate causes before mitigations. For each material scenario, identify the exposed assumption/dependency, earliest warning, counterevidence and existing safeguards.

Choose the smallest adequate disposition: remove the exposure, revise approach/sequence, resolve uncertainty, add required proof/recovery, or retain a justified residual risk. A mitigation needs an owner and checkable completion; retained operational risk needs an observable stop/re-entry trigger where applicable. Update the owning plan and affected acceptance/dependencies. Mitigation does not expand scope or authority.

The affected plan remains unready while a material threat, required decision/evidence, or freshness gap persists. Residual risk must sit within accepted constraints and the responsible decision-maker's authority; it cannot relabel an unmet requirement or missing mandatory proof. Recheck only affected analysis after a material change, including failure paths introduced by a mitigation.

Record the plan boundary, material scenarios and dispositions, changes, and readiness conclusion in the existing plan or conversation. A supported no-change result is valid; do not create a separate risk ledger. Premortem reasoning is not runtime proof.
