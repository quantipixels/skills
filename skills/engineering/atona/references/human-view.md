# Initiative human view

Read when an Atọ́nà initiative maintains a browser projection because separate records, lifecycle depth, or multi-session work would otherwise make conscious human judgment expensive.

The semantic plan and specialist results remain authoritative. The projection is a denormalized Initiative Assurance View: it must expose the meaning a human needs to judge the current gate without forcing record archaeology.

## Pin the viewpoint

Use one primary viewpoint by default:

```text
Stakeholder / reader
Current concern
Judgment/action they must make
Current initiative gate
Evidence cut
```

Create another view only when a materially different stakeholder concern would otherwise distort or overload the primary view. Do not manufacture approver/implementer/reviewer dashboards by taxonomy.

## Reach human-critical closure

A unit is human-critical when omission could materially change the reader's judgment of:

- outcome, acceptance, scope, or non-goals;
- current gate or plan/process viability;
- consequential decision, alternative, assumption, or revisit trigger;
- behavior/operating contract sufficiency;
- architecture/design condition or readiness gap;
- delivery candidate/state, dependency, blocker, or cumulative drift;
- proof strength, counterevidence, freshness, or unproved behavior;
- material risk, reversibility, rollback/recovery, or failure condition; or
- exact next owner/action required to proceed.

Give these obligations to `html-artifact`. Human-critical meaning must be visible with source identity; a bare link/pointer is insufficient. Do not copy whole linked records when concise decision-relevant meaning plus provenance closes the obligation.

## First viewport: make the gate legible

Lead with the initiative's current judgment surface, not record navigation:

```text
Outcome
Status
Current gate
Gate posture / blocking condition
Decision Frontier
Delivery summary
Weakest material claim or critical blocker
Next action
Material change since the previous useful projection
```

Use a small assurance-state vocabulary only when it helps distinguish claims:

```text
SUPPORTED
CONDITIONAL
BLOCKED
UNPROVED
STALE
NOT_APPLICABLE
```

These states come from exact-current semantic owners and Atọ́nà's gate integration; HTML cannot invent them.

## Keep evaluation semantics distinct

- **Verdict** states what evidence establishes.
- **Confidence** states strength/limits of evidence or judgment; prefer `High | Medium | Low` unless a numeric model has real meaning.
- **Comparative grade/score** is allowed only against an explicit rubric for viable alternatives.
- **Gate** is mandatory and cannot be averaged away.
- **Readiness** states whether progression is currently justified.

Never summarize the initiative as one percentage/health score. If one unproved recovery obligation blocks progression, foreground that weakest material claim even when several other claims are supported.

When several credible alternatives remain and at least two independent criteria can materially change the choice, a compact rubric may aid comparison. Apply hard gates first; derive the few criteria from the actual decision; show the decisive factor and meaningful counterevidence/alternative rather than ranking trivia.

## Trace acceptance through proof

When material, expose the current chain:

```text
Outcome / acceptance
→ decision / contract
→ architecture / owner
→ delivery candidate
→ proof
```

Also challenge backward from material implementation/proof. Surface owner-established gaps equivalent to:

- requirement orphan — accepted obligation lacks adequate implementation/proof;
- implementation orphan — material mechanism lacks accepted contract/decision basis;
- proof orphan — evidence no longer establishes a current material claim.

Do not infer semantic orphan status solely from projection structure; consume the relevant owner result/evidence.

## Preserve a coherent evidence cut

A visually current page must not silently combine mutually stale sources. Pin the exact Atọ́nà revision and every material owner result/candidate/proof the projection uses. If a child result was produced against superseded plan/contract/candidate state, show it as `STALE`/partial or refresh it before using it to support a current assurance claim.

## Delta first for living initiatives

After the first complete projection, foreground only material delta before unchanged detail:

```text
+ newly confirmed decision/result/proof
- superseded or stale evidence
! newly blocking/conditional claim
~ materially changed scope/gate/next action
```

Do not make the reader reread the entire initiative to discover what changed.

## Progressive disclosure

Keep current gate/outcome, blocking/conditional/unproved/stale claims, next action, and decision-changing constraints visible. Put important rationale/alternatives/counterevidence one action away. Keep exhaustive packets/logs/history at canonical source locators.

Use `html-artifact` to choose representation per material relationship rather than copying this outline into cards. A decision trace may be a graph, a state may be a state diagram, a change may be a shape-aware diff, quantitative evidence may be a chart, and a short condition may remain prose.

## Projection closure proof

Before presenting the projection as current, require:

- every human-critical obligation has visible decision-relevant meaning and canonical provenance;
- the evidence cut is coherent or every stale/partial dependency is visible;
- the weakest material claim/blocker and next action are discoverable without source archaeology;
- hard gates are not obscured by aggregate scores or positive evidence elsewhere;
- material counterevidence/conditions remain visible at the resolution needed for the gate;
- source epistemic status (`Observed | Inferred | Proposed | Illustrative`) is preserved where presentation could otherwise increase apparent authority; and
- the projection's claimed interaction/visual correctness is proved only to the level required by HTML Artifact.

Keep this proof agent-native unless recurring mechanical omissions demonstrate a deterministic verifier would materially improve correctness.
