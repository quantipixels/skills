# Experimental skill disposition

Read only when a portfolio audit must decide whether an Experimental skill should be promoted, kept experimental, narrowed, folded, replaced, or removed.

## Require real-use evidence

Kọ Skill does not infer historical usage from repository structure, router metadata, or current package state. Consume verified evidence supplied by the user, `ayewo-igba-ise` corpus analysis, Skill Doctor, or another source whose population, availability, and evidence boundary are explicit.

Useful evidence may include:

- eligible-opportunity denominator;
- selected, missed, mis-triggered, or unavailable cases;
- observed incremental value and recurring cost;
- boundary/authority failures or healthy composition;
- counterevidence; and
- coverage limitations.

Treat missing historical evidence as an evidence gap. Do not infer recurrence, invocation frequency, missed opportunity, or incremental value from source structure. Low raw invocation count is not failure when the owned outcome is genuinely rare.

## Judge capability and public identity separately

Use real-use evidence together with the exact-current package and portfolio to judge:

- whether the capability remains useful;
- whether its outcome remains independently useful and distinct;
- whether trigger, authority, cost, and adjacent-owner boundaries are healthy;
- whether its separate public identity materially improves direct selection/use compared with folding the capability behind another owner; and
- whether observed value/cost supports changing maturity or shape.

Capability success does not by itself justify `PROMOTE`. If folding preserves the observed benefit while reducing public/routing surface, prefer `FOLD`.

## Disposition

Use one when the evidence supports it:

- `PROMOTE` — representative real-use evidence shows distinct recurring value, healthy boundaries, justified cost, and useful independent public selection;
- `KEEP_EXPERIMENTING` — the boundary remains credible but real-use/public-identity evidence is insufficient;
- `NARROW` — useful behavior is proved but trigger/scope is broader than justified;
- `FOLD` — useful behavior belongs inside another owner and public identity adds unnecessary surface;
- `REPLACE` — the capability hypothesis remains useful but this public shape is not the best vehicle; or
- `REMOVE` — current structural or real-use evidence proves redundancy, harm, mis-triggering, or unjustified recurring cost.

`PROMOTE` cannot be justified from structure alone. Structural defects may still justify `NARROW`, `FOLD`, `REPLACE`, or `REMOVE` without historical use evidence when the defect itself is proved.

Never invoke an experiment merely to manufacture graduation evidence. One success does not authorize removal or narrowing of a stable owner by itself.

Return the evidence packet consumed, missing evidence, structural/boundary assessment, public-identity assessment, and disposition only to the extent the evidence supports them.
