# Adapt the project's development lifecycle

Read when establishing project practice, choosing consequential lifecycle depth or correcting demonstrated process friction. Reuse sufficient methods; commands retain their results and authority. Ordinary work needs no setup ceremony or full lifecycle audit.

## Recover the project philosophy

Recover purpose, beneficiaries, success and consequential trade-offs from relevant instructions, standards, architecture, domain records, non-goals and accepted decisions. Trace a representative change when needed to understand actual practice; use [sawari](../../commands/sawari.md) for source gaps. Distinguish governing intent, observed habits, inference and unknown motivation. Repetition does not establish a principle, nor absence an exclusion.

Use [arojinle](../../commands/arojinle.md) with the actual user for consequential unconfirmed priorities or conflicts. Bring evidence and concrete trade-offs involving relevant quality, project lifetime, compatibility, operational burden, cost or human involvement. Ask what must hold and what outcome justifies extra complexity; reuse settled answers. The [agent-respondent perspective](../productivity/inquiry/agent-respondent.md) can challenge the evidence map, not invent the user's philosophy. If the user is unavailable, retain the controlling gap and continue independent work.

Record confirmed principles, rationale and applicability in existing README, design or contributor guidance. Standards, architecture decisions, domain terms and exclusions keep their owners. A short philosophy section can suffice; no default `PHILOSOPHY.md` is required. Distinguish binding outcomes and invariants from preferences and experiments, including when defaults may change.

## Fit the whole SDLC to the outcome

Locate the first unresolved result and the feedback that will establish it. Consider the whole lifecycle when adapting project practice, while entering only the parts needed for the task and its requested finish:

| Lifecycle concern | Agreement, capability or evidence that matters |
| --- | --- |
| Frame the problem | Beneficiary, need, scope, domain meaning and observable success; distinguish an accepted goal from a proposed mechanism. |
| Shape and design | Settle consequential behavior, structure and uncertainty enough for the next useful change; use research or a prototype only for a decision it can resolve. |
| Implement and test | A usable workspace, tools and relevant knowledge; bounded changes with fast, meaningful proof and a way to recover from a failed attempt. |
| Verify and review | Changed-contract and assembled-system evidence, independent judgment where required, and corrections completed locally before publication. |
| Publish and release | Actual delivery authority, compatibility and migration obligations, release evidence and the applicable rollback or forward-recovery path. |
| Operate and recover | Observable live outcomes, useful diagnostics, support ownership and a recovery path where the project operates a live system. Local green does not prove live correctness. |
| Learn and adapt | User, operational and work-session feedback that changes a decision, capability, check or method, with evidence for retaining the improvement. |

Use the [command menu and playbooks](../../SKILL.md#commands) for result owners and the [reference guide](../README.md) for depth and learned workflows. The table is coverage guidance, not mandatory phases or documents. Scale depth to outcome, uncertainty, exposure, reversibility and maintenance cost. A formatting repair may need a focused check and diff inspection; a data migration may need design, stateful proof, review, release observation and recovery evidence. Preserve required checks and review; reuse settled decisions and proof.

## Give the agent room to change course

Within existing authority, choose, revise and undo owned changes as evidence warrants. Preserve unrelated work and establish a proportionate recovery boundary before consequential experiments, using existing branches, snapshots, fixtures or rollback tools as needed. A source revert does not undo external effects or restore lost data. Planned intermediate breakage stays isolated and recoverable until the relevant integration boundary.

Supply missing knowledge or capability that blocks good work: useful logs, a test path, fixtures, documentation, a tool or specialist. Setup grants no new access, installation or live-effect authority. The agent can change implementation details while preserving confirmed intent, invariants and proof. A disproved hypothesis calls for correction, without another permission round for already authorized work.

Judge complexity against the outcome and credible failures. Prefer capable existing mechanisms; added abstractions, services, scripts or process must address an actual obligation or evidenced recurring cost. Consider under-solving as well as maintenance burden; fewer lines or greater generality alone proves neither fitness nor simplicity.

## Codify what earns a durable home

Repair the level causing the problem: product/tool interface, deterministic enforcement of a mechanical invariant, or judgment guidance in standards/review. Keep navigation pointers at the agent entrypoint. Scripts and gates need a valuable repeated operation or detectable failure. Remove superseded reminders when their required scope is covered.

For a changed practice, state its expected benefit and the smallest representative check that could disprove it. Exercise the affected path within authority; retain unexecuted gaps. Use relevant outcomes such as local defect detection, feedback time, rework and maintainability rather than counting steps or files. Revisit changed assumptions or poor results through [ayewo-retro](../../commands/ayewo-retro.md); retain qualified [learned workflows](../productivity/learned-workflows.md). No mandatory report or metric store is needed.
