# Pruning human prose

Read only when the user explicitly asks to shorten prose, reduce verbosity, remove repetition, or lower reading load.

Pin the reader, purpose, language, voice, required structure, technical familiarity, and content that must remain exact. Preserve facts, evidence, citations, decisions, uncertainty, identifiers, accepted terminology, required fields, and enough conceptual scaffolding for the reader to reason correctly.

Shorter is not automatically better. Remove load that does not help the reader; do not make the reader reconstruct missing context, causality, or authority.

## Prune in this order

1. Remove filler, throat-clearing, repeated conclusions, and summaries that add no fact or action.
2. Merge explanations with genuinely identical meaning.
3. Remove formulaic examples or rhetorical variants that neither replace explanation nor prevent a plausible misunderstanding.
4. Remove rationale that does not help the reader decide or understand a non-obvious boundary.
5. Move background only when the artifact has a clear linked home and the main reader job remains intact.
6. Replace long constructions with shorter familiar ones while preserving precise technical terms.
7. Repair rhythm, transitions, and sentence structure after the cuts.

Keep repetition when it serves a different reader need, warning, exception, decision boundary, or independent verification point.

## Do not prune away technical adequacy

A concept can be brief and still complete. Before removing explanatory material, ask whether the intended reader still has what they need to understand:

- prerequisites and dependencies;
- mechanism or causal relationship;
- units, state, actor, scope, and important conditions;
- why a non-obvious constraint exists; and
- the distinction between similar technical concepts when confusing them would change action or judgment.

A familiar audience can carry more implicit context. An unfamiliar audience needs more scaffolding. Do not optimize both to the same word count.

## Verify the result

Compare the final prose with the exact baseline.

- Confirm that facts, argument/procedure, citations, uncertainty, identifiers, decisions, and required structure remain correct.
- Confirm that the intended reader can still follow the technical concepts without guessing at a missing bridge.
- Confirm that every retained example or rationale still earns its space.
- Confirm that the voice still fits the artifact.

Use before/after word count only as a description of the change, not proof of quality. For material cuts, report meaningful content moved/removed and any preservation limit.

Return the pruned prose first, then only material count, meaning, evidence, technical-adequacy, or voice notes.
