# Orthography, àmì and pronunciation

Read for spelling, tone marking, pronunciation notation or correction. This is a Standard Yorùbá foundation, not a licence to regularize dialects or personal names.

## Distinctions that matter

Standard writing distinguishes seven oral vowel letters: `a e ẹ i o ọ u`. Underdots in `ẹ`, `ọ` and `ṣ` distinguish letters/sounds; they are not tone marks. `gb` represents one consonant sound. Nasal vowels and syllabic nasals are different: a following `n` can mark vowel nasalization rather than a separately pronounced consonant; a syllabic nasal can itself bear tone. Typical native syllable patterns are V, CV and syllabic N. Do not apply these mechanically to untranslated identifiers or foreign names. [1]

**Àmì ohùn** represent tone: high uses acute, low uses grave, and mid is normally unmarked. Mid tone is still a tone. Tone can distinguish words: `bí` (give birth), `bi` (ask), `bì` (vomit). Teach low–mid–high as relative pitch, not fixed musical notes or English stress. [2]

## Apply the distinctions

Check letters/underdots separately from tone. Meaning and grammatical context control the reading; accents cannot reliably be reconstructed from bare letters alone. Do not replace a lexical-tone explanation with a punctuation or stress explanation. Treat contractions, vowel sequences and connected-speech tone changes as contextual forms to verify, not a mechanical accent-copying exercise.

Use Unicode NFC for authored text and check that underdots and tone marks survive copying/rendering; normalization does not supply missing marks. Preserve source quotations and identifiers exactly, recording proposed corrections separately.

For pronunciation, use verified audio when available. Label text-only guidance as approximate and do not claim to assess a learner's spoken tone from spelling. Older PDFs and web pages may use legacy fonts: inspect the rendered form or another reliable source before copying a suspect glyph.
