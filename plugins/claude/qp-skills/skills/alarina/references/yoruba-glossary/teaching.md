# Teach mode

Teach the requested Yorùbá topic directly. Use the learner's stated ability, goal and preferred explanation language; otherwise begin with an accessible example and adjust from their response. Ask only for context that would materially change the lesson, such as a requested dialect or pronunciation target.

Keep a lesson focused, usually one to three related concepts:

- explain the rule in plain language;
- show a short, accurately marked example and its meaning, using a contrast when that makes the distinction visible;
- give a small task that requires using the rule, then respond to the learner's attempt with a corrected form and the reason;
- reuse earlier material when it helps reveal or repair a misunderstanding.

Separate spelling, tone, pronunciation, grammar and register in feedback. A learner who understands spoken Yorùbá may need literacy practice rather than beginner conversation. Accept legitimate variants; do not grade a dialect as wrong merely because it differs from the selected standard.

For tone practice, compare words with the same letters and different tones, mark the syllables being contrasted, and use verified audio where available. Text pitch descriptions cannot establish how the learner actually pronounced a word. Do not claim audio assessment without audio access.

Teach word creation through transparent, attested formations before inviting proposals; explicitly label the learner's or model's coinages. Teaching an established term does not require project approval, and practice answers do not become glossary entries automatically.

Finish a single explanation when it answers the question. For an interactive lesson, leave one useful practice task and continue from the response. Preserve learning progress in the conversation; create/update a durable course or glossary only when requested. Use [html-artifact](../../commands/html-artifact.md) for an actual learning plan and its living progress view, not as a compulsory artifact for every short lesson.

Pedagogical design here is a practical skill workflow, not a proven optimal syllabus. The University of Texas at Austin's [Yorùbá Yé Mi](https://coerll.utexas.edu/yemi/index.php) supplies contextual lessons and verified listening material; link to relevant units rather than copying whole lessons or exercises.

For science-focused learners, [Science in Yorùbá’s video archive](https://scienceinyoruba.org/tao/) offers topics for contextual language practice. Inspect the selected explanation before building a lesson around it; distinguish its emerging terminology from settled usage and assess audio only when accessible.
