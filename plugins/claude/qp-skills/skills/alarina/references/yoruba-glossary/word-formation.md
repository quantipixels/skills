# Word formation and proposed terminology

Read for explaining how words are built or choosing a new term. Word formation is linguistic evidence; adopting a technical label is a separate decision.

## Established processes

Yorùbá nominal formation uses prefixation, reduplication and compounding. For example, `ì-` plus `fẹ́` yields `ìfẹ́` (“love” as a noun). Tone and the category/meaning of the base matter; a prefix is not a universal translation of an English suffix. Full and partial reduplication have different distributions and functions. Compounds need not mean the simple sum of their parts. Their boundaries may retain both vowels or undergo vowel elision or assimilation; these changes are not automatic. Verify the attested form. [1]

Descriptions of affixation differ across analyses: Eleshin limits affixation to prefixation, while Pulleyblank discusses an “infixing reduplication” category. Do not turn this terminological/theoretical difference into a universal ban on every intervening element. Check the construction being discussed. [1,2]

For a common gerundive pattern, a copy of the initial consonant plus high `í` precedes the base: `tà → títà` (sell → selling), `lò → lílò` (use → using). This is partial reduplication, not a general instruction to repeat any word. [3]

Allomorphy changes the surface form of a morpheme with its environment. For instance, the `oní-` family includes forms such as `alá-` and `elé-`; verify the whole derived word rather than joining strings. `oní + ilé → onílé` illustrates a conventional formation. [4]

## Choosing a term

The following is a practical editorial method, not an official naming standard:

1. Establish the concept, intended users and grammatical role. Search attested specialist and general usage before coining a replacement.
2. Prefer a clear existing term when it fits. Otherwise compare a descriptive phrase, a supported derivation/compound, semantic extension or an adapted borrowing. Borrowing is not inherently wrong; avoid unfamiliar coinage merely to exclude English.
3. For a candidate, explain its components, marked pronunciation, intended sense, evidence and possible competing meanings. Verify that the formation works in natural sentences and related expressions; concatenating dictionary entries is insufficient.
4. Label unattested formations as proposed. Linguistic plausibility, frequency, speaker acceptance and formal standardization are different evidence. Seek a competent speaker or specialist judgment when a consequential public term remains uncertain; otherwise keep the uncertainty visible.

Do not invent native etymologies for borrowed words or confidently assign tones by analogy. Keep code/API/product identifiers exact. If a Yorùbá term is unsettled, retain the English technical label with an explanation while presenting candidates.

Use [glossary maintenance](glossary.md) only when actual term decisions or authorized glossary changes are requested. Reusing an established word needs no approval round.

For scientific concepts, consult [Science in Yorùbá’s emerging vocabulary](https://scienceinyoruba.org/new-yoruba-words/) and relevant explanatory articles. The list identifies itself as selections from Adebayo’s work in progress. Record the exact entry, intended sense and source status; attestation there establishes the author’s usage, not language-wide standardization. Compare other specialist usage before adopting a disputed term.
